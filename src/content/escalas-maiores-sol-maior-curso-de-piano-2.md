This is the second lesson in my piano-course series. After starting with C major, we move to **G major**.

G major has one sharp: **F♯**. Its notes are:

**G – A – B – C – D – E – F♯ – G**

[Watch the original G-major lesson on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9MxfbWe3IY)

## What the lesson covers

In the video, I play the scale over two octaves and demonstrate the fingering. At the end, I also play the C-major, D-major, and G-major chords.

Together, those chords are the IV, V, and I chords in G major. Playing them after the scale helps connect the notes you are practicing with the harmony you will hear in songs.

![G-major scale and fingering from the original lesson](/blog-images/legacy/2016/06/12-Escalas-Maiores-Piano-2016-06-16-2036_0001-e1466256419779-1024x289.png)

## How to practice

Start with one hand at a time. Keep the F♯ consistent, especially when descending. Once each hand feels comfortable, combine them at a tempo that lets you play evenly.

The next goal is not speed: it is a smooth thumb crossing and a steady pulse across both octaves.
