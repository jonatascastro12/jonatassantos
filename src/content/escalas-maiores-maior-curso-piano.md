I began my piano-course series with major scales, starting with **C major**. Learning scales makes it easier to recognize patterns and play music in different keys.

C major uses the white keys:

**C – D – E – F – G – A – B – C**

[Watch the original C-major lesson on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zspP0Q5tCJs)

## Practice one hand at a time

Learn the right-hand fingering and the left-hand fingering separately. Put the hands together once you can play each part comfortably.

Use a metronome to support a steady pulse. The original lesson suggested starting around 60–65 beats per minute and increasing by two beats at a time. Treat that as a suggestion: choose a slower starting tempo if you need it, and increase only when the passage is comfortable and even.

Pay attention to two forms of consistency:

- **Timing:** notes should sit evenly against the pulse.
- **Sound:** aim for an even tone across the scale, including the thumb crossings.

You do not need a dedicated metronome; an app can provide the same basic reference.

## A book that helped me

Hanon's *The Virtuoso Pianist in 60 Exercises* helped me during my own piano studies. Exercises can be useful, provided you play thoughtfully and keep your hands relaxed.

## Correction to the original video

I made a mistake in the video when explaining finger numbers. I described the little finger of the left hand as finger 1. The convention is the same for both hands:

| Finger | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Thumb | 1 |
| Index | 2 |
| Middle | 3 |
| Ring | 4 |
| Little finger | 5 |

The left-hand little finger is therefore **5**, and the right-hand thumb is **1**.
