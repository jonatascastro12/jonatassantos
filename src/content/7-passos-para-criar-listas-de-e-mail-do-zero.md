Why build an email list when you already have a blog or social-media account?

A list gives interested readers a way to keep hearing from you directly. You can share new articles, continue a conversation, and introduce a product when it is relevant to the subscriber.

In the original post, I introduced a short ebook about starting an email list from zero. Its goals were to help readers promote their blog posts, communicate with their audience, and build a more consistent relationship over time.

## A practical starting point

The useful foundation is simple: give people a clear reason to subscribe, explain what they will receive, and make leaving the list easy.

Before building elaborate automations, answer these questions:

- Who is the newsletter for?
- What useful material will they receive?
- How often will it arrive?
- Where can they subscribe and unsubscribe?
- How will you tell whether the messages are useful?

A small list of interested readers is a better starting point than a large collection of addresses from people who never asked to hear from you.

## What changed in this edition

The 2016 article cited email-usage and return-on-investment statistics from Viver de Blog. Those figures were part of the original promotion; their underlying studies are not available here, so I have not repeated them as current evidence or promised a particular financial return.

The durable point is the relationship: send something useful, set expectations, and evaluate replies and meaningful engagement rather than assuming that a growing address count guarantees sales.
